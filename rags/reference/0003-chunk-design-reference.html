Chunk Design Reference

A compact decision sheet for choosing the retrieval grain in baseline RAG. Use it before tuning prompts, top-k, rerankers, or GraphRAG.

Size

Controls precision. Smaller chunks reduce noise, but can lose the surrounding idea. Larger chunks preserve context, but can bury the answer.

Overlap

Protects boundaries. Overlap helps when answers straddle chunk edges, but increases duplicate storage, embedding cost, and repeated context.

Metadata

Controls routing. Metadata such as source, title path, date, permission, customer, product, and jurisdiction lets retrieval filter before ranking.

Chunk Grain Algorithm

1. Name the question shape: fact, clause, procedure, row lookup, comparison, or summary. 2. Pick the smallest semantic unit that can answer that shape. 3. Attach metadata needed for filters, citations, permissions, freshness, and debugging. 4. Add overlap only where boundary splits cause misses. 5. Test retrieval with representative questions before changing prompts.

Rule: a chunk is the modeled grain of retrieval, not a fixed number of characters.

Symptom Map

Retrieval SymptomFirst Chunking MoveWatch Out For
Correct chunk retrieved, but answer is buried in noiseSplit smaller or use better structural boundariesDo not split away definitions, headings, or units of meaning.
Correct document retrieved, wrong passage selectedAlign chunks to semantic sections, not arbitrary windowsOverlapping bad boundaries can preserve the bad boundary.
Answer straddles two neighboring chunksAdd overlap or preserve parent contextToo much overlap can crowd top-k with near-duplicates.
Chunks look relevant but ignore customer, date, or jurisdictionAdd metadata and filtersSemantic similarity cannot enforce business constraints by itself.
Good retrieval is too expensiveReduce duplication, use stable IDs, and persist embeddingsDo not trade away recall without measuring it.

Senior habit: when chunking changes, re-run retrieval evaluation. A cleaner-looking chunking scheme can silently lower recall.
